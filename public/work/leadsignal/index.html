The honest test of a firm selling AI systems is not a deck. It is whether they have put a real product into production, taken money through it, and kept it running long after the interesting part was over.

LeadSignal is that test, done in public. It handles other businesses’ customer conversations, holds their data, takes their payments and answers to a platform’s app review. There is nowhere in it to hide a shortcut, which is exactly why it is the work we point at.

What got built

Not a demo. A product other businesses run their sales on.

A multi-tenant platform

Every business gets its own isolated workspace, data, users, settings and billing. Tenancy is the hardest thing to retrofit and the thing most internal AI projects discover far too late, so it went in first rather than last.

An agent architecture

Separate agents for conversation, support, marketing and outreach, each with its own knowledge, its own voice rules and its own limits. They score, reply, follow up and hand back to a human on rules written down in advance rather than on a hunch.

Live payments and platform approval

Real subscriptions, real invoices, real failed-card handling, and an app that passed review to hold production messaging permissions. The unglamorous half of shipping, and the half that separates a product from a prototype.

Built with this method

The eight weeks, week by week.

This is the sequence we use on client systems, run at full speed on our own. Look at where the weeks actually went: scope and data before anything clever, production traffic early, billing and review before launch rather than after it.

Week 1

Requirements, before a line of code

What a lead is. What the agent may say, and where it must stop and fetch a human. The data model the whole thing hangs off. The same conversation we charge clients for, because skipping it is what makes month seven expensive.

Weeks 2–3

The spine

Tenancy, authentication, the lead record and the CRM around it. Boring, load-bearing, and the part that decides whether everything after it is cheap or painful to change.

Weeks 3–5

Channels, in production early

Instagram, Facebook, WhatsApp, SMS and X wired in and taking live webhooks against real accounts. Integrations are where these projects actually fail, so they were proven on real traffic rather than on a mock at the end.

Weeks 5–6

The agents and the guardrails

Scoring, drafting in the client’s voice, follow-up timing, opt-out handling, quiet hours and the handback rules. The guardrails were built alongside the capability, not bolted on after it misbehaved.

Week 7

Billing and entitlements

Plans, subscriptions, trials, and what each tier may switch on. The point at which a build becomes a business, and the point most internal AI projects never reach.

Week 8

Review, hardening, monitoring

Platform app review, rate limits, failure handling, and alerting that tells us a seam has come apart before a customer does. Then live, with paying tenants on it.
