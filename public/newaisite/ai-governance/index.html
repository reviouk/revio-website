Who needs this

Anyone whose AI touches a customer or a record.

If the system reads personal data, writes to a customer, or makes a decision that affects one, somebody will eventually ask what it was allowed to do and what it actually did. Answering that afterwards is expensive. Deciding it beforehand is a morning's work.

It matters most for regulated buyers, for anyone selling into enterprise procurement, and for anyone whose insurer or board has started asking.

What you get

Documents that match the system.

What is included

  • A written statement of what each agent may see, do and say, and where it must hand to a person
  • Data flow mapping — what leaves your systems, where it goes, and who processes it
  • The disclosure rules: when a customer is told they are talking to AI, and in which markets
  • Retention and opt-out handling, wired into the system rather than promised in a policy
  • An audit trail: what the AI did, when, and on whose instruction

What is not

Straight answers

Questions people ask before booking.

Are you lawyers?

No, and we will say so in writing. This is the engineering half: making the system behave the way your policy says it does, and producing the evidence. Your solicitor should read the result, and we will hand it over in a form they can.

Does this cover the EU AI Act?

It covers the parts that touch the build — transparency when a person is talking to AI, records of what it did, and human oversight. Where an obligation is genuinely legal rather than technical, we will tell you that it needs advice rather than code.

Can we buy it on its own?

Yes, though most people buy it with a build, where it is cheaper because the decisions are being made anyway.
