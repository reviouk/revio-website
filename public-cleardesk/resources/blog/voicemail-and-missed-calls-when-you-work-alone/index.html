What to say on your voicemail and what to do about missed calls.

When you work alone, some calls will go unanswered. Here is how to make that fine.

More from the blog

If you work alone, you cannot answer every call. You are up a ladder, under a sink, with a client or driving. That is normal.

What matters is what the caller hears, and how soon you get back to them. Both are easy to get right.

What a good voicemail says.

Keep it short and warm. A good greeting does five things.

  • Says who they have reached: your name and your business.
  • Says you cannot answer right now, without a long excuse.
  • Asks for what you need: name, number and a few words about the job.
  • Says when you will call back.
  • Sounds like you.

Greetings you can copy.

"Hello, you have reached Alex at Brook Plumbing. I am on a job at the moment. Please leave your name, your number and a few words about what you need, and I will call you back this afternoon."
"Hi, this is Alex. I am away until Monday 14th. Leave your name, number and what you need, and I will ring you on Monday. For anything urgent, please call Sam on 07700 900123."

Change the names, days and number to suit. Use the second kind when you are away, and put it back when you return.

Handling missed calls.

Pick set times to check your phone, for example at lunch and at the end of the day. Call people back in the order they rang. Write down what each caller wanted before you dial, so you sound prepared.

If you cannot talk, a quick text is better than silence. It tells the caller you have seen the call and when to expect you.

A text you can send.

"Hi, this is Alex from Brook Plumbing. Sorry I missed your call. I am on a job right now. I will ring you this afternoon. If it is quicker, text me what you need."

If you call back and get no answer, leave one short message and send a text. Then leave it there. They know how to reach you.

What to leave out.

Skip the long list of options, the apology and the sales pitch. A caller who is leaving a message wants to know that you will get it, and when you will reply. Record in a quiet place, so your voice is easy to hear.

Avoid promising a call back at a time you cannot keep. "This afternoon" or "by the end of the day" is easier to meet than "within the hour".

Calling back well.

When you ring back, say your name and your business first, then why you are calling: "You rang earlier about a leaking tap." Ask the questions you need, agree the next step, and say what happens next. If the job is not one you can take, say so kindly and, if you can, suggest where they might look.

Good habits.

  • Ring your own number from another phone and listen to the greeting.
  • Keep the message current. Update it for holidays and busy periods.
  • Keep it brief. Callers want to leave a message, not hear a speech.
  • Make sure your voicemail box is not full.
Quick answers

Quick answers.

How long should a voicemail greeting be?

Short enough to be said comfortably in one breath or two. If you can cut a sentence, cut it.

Should I use my name or the business name?

Use both. Your name is friendly and the business name confirms they have the right number.
