Why this exists

A roadmap nobody has time to build is just an expensive document.

The usual shape of AI consultancy is advice: a strategy, a roadmap, a set of recommendations, an invoice. Then the work lands on a team that already has a job, and in six months nothing on the roadmap has been built.

The other usual shape is hiring — an AI lead at a salary a business your size cannot justify for a problem that may take two days a week. This is the middle: senior judgement and the hands to act on it, for a fraction of a hire, every month, with the work visible.

How a month runs

Prioritise, build, review, measure.

Prioritise

We agree what the month is for, in your order, against what it is worth. Everything else stays on the backlog where you can see it.

Build

The work gets done — mostly remote, on site when being in the room is genuinely faster, and always against your real systems rather than a sandbox.

Review

An hour at the end of the month on what shipped, what it changed, and what the numbers say. Where something did not work, that is on the list too.

Measure

Each thing we build carries a number it is supposed to move. If it does not move it, we say so and we take it out rather than leaving it running quietly.

Straight answers

Questions people ask before booking.

How is this different from hiring somebody?

A senior AI lead costs considerably more than this as a salary, before recruitment, and most businesses your size do not have five days a week of work for one. This is the same judgement at the fraction you actually need, and it starts in a fortnight rather than in a quarter.

How is it different from an advisor or a fractional CTO?

The delivery capacity comes with it. An advisor tells you what to build. This includes building it, which is the part that usually does not happen.

Who actually does the work?

The person you meet. Not a scoping partner who hands you to somebody junior once the contract is signed.

Can we start with the audit and decide after?

That is the usual route, and the one we would recommend. The audit gives you the backlog this service would work through — and if it turns out there is only one thing worth doing, a fixed-price build is the cheaper answer and we will tell you.

What if a month is quiet?

It carries. What we will not do is invent work to fill the days — a quiet month is a conversation about whether you still need this, and we would rather have that conversation than bill through it.

Do you work on site?

Some of it, where being in the room is genuinely faster — training, workshops, the first look inside a messy system. The rest is remote, which is how most of it gets done.
