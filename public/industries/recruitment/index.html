Where this fits

The placement is the job. The paperwork around it is not.

A recruitment or staffing agency's value is in the placement, the judgement about whether a candidate is right for a client. What surrounds that judgement is a lot of repetitive work: parsing hundreds of CVs against a spec, writing the same first-pass candidate message, drafting a job ad from a brief, chasing interview times across two diaries, and keeping a CRM clean enough to be useful. CV parsing and matching, candidate comms and screening, job-ad writing, interview scheduling and CRM hygiene are the five jobs AI does well here.

Bias is the risk that has to be named up front. A model trained or prompted carelessly can quietly favour or exclude candidates in ways that would breach the Equality Act, so any screening or matching tool we build is scoped to surface candidates against the objective criteria in the brief, not to reject anyone outright, and a consultant makes the final call on every shortlist. We would rather turn down a build than ship something that automates a discrimination risk.

Fit

Who this is for, and where we say wait.

A good fit

  • Recruitment and staffing agencies with a CRM or ATS in place
  • Enough volume that CV screening or comms genuinely eats consultant time
  • A willingness to keep a consultant signing off every shortlist
  • A director who wants the bias risk managed properly, not ignored

Not this, yet

  • You want AI to reject candidates without a human review
  • There is no digital candidate record to work from at all
  • Volume is low enough that manual screening is genuinely faster
  • You need this live in days rather than weeks

Straight answers

Questions agencies ask before they start.

Could AI screening breach the Equality Act?

It can, if it is built carelessly. We scope matching and screening tools to rank candidates against the objective criteria in the brief, never to reject anyone outright, and a consultant makes the final shortlist decision every time.

Will this replace consultants?

No. It removes the repetitive first pass, parsing, screening and scheduling, so consultants spend their time on the calls and relationships that actually place people.

Can it connect to our ATS or CRM?

Most UK recruitment CRMs and ATS platforms have an API or export we can work with. This is checked in the audit before anything is quoted.

What happens if a candidate objects to being screened by AI?

The screening step is disclosed and a human review is always available; nothing final happens without a consultant's sign-off.

How do you keep candidate data safe?

Data handling is mapped before anything is built, covering what a system can see, where CVs and personal data are stored, and how long they are kept.

Do you work with contingency and retained agencies differently?

The audit adapts to your model. Retained searches usually lean on screening and client reporting; high-volume contingency work usually leans on parsing and CRM hygiene.

How quickly could a first automation be live?

A first build, usually CV matching or screening, is typically live within six weeks of the audit finishing, running unsupervised within nine.
