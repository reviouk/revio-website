How to chase an unpaid invoice politely.

A calm order of steps, and wording you can copy and use today.

More from the blog

Chasing money can feel awkward. It is also a normal part of running a business. A clear, friendly message is usually the right place to start, and it keeps the relationship intact.

Here is an order that works for a sole trader or a small business, with words you can copy.

Check the basics first.

Before you chase, make sure the invoice reached the right person. Is the email address correct? Does the invoice show the amount, the due date and how to pay? Does the customer need a purchase order number on it?

Often an invoice has simply been missed or has landed in the wrong inbox. Starting from that thought keeps your tone friendly. It also means that if the mistake was on your side, you can put it right without any awkwardness.

Keep a simple list of what you have invoiced and when it falls due. A glance at it once a week tells you which invoices need a nudge.

A simple order of steps.

1. A friendly nudge on or just before the due date. Optional, but it works well with new customers.

2. A gentle reminder just after the due date. Assume it slipped through.

3. A clear follow-up about a week later. Name a date you would like to be paid by.

4. A phone call. Many problems are solved in one conversation.

5. A final message. State plainly what you will do next.

Words you can copy.

First reminder

"Hi Sam, a quick note that invoice 1042 for the kitchen repair was due on Friday. I have attached it again in case it got lost. Could you let me know when it will be paid? Thank you."

Follow-up

"Hi Sam, I wanted to check on invoice 1042. It is now overdue. Could you pay it by Friday 12th? The bank details are on the invoice. If there is a problem with it, please tell me and I will sort it out."

On the phone

"Hello Sam, it is Alex. I am calling about invoice 1042. I wanted to check it reached you and ask when I can expect payment."

Final message

"Hi Sam, I have not yet received payment for invoice 1042, which was due on the 5th. Please pay by Friday 19th. If I do not hear from you, I will need to look at other ways to recover it. I would much rather sort this out together."

Change the names, numbers and dates to suit. These are examples, not legal wording.

Tone tips.

  • Stick to facts: the invoice number, the amount and the due date.
  • Say what you would like to happen, and by when.
  • Make it easy to pay. Put your bank details or payment link in the message.
  • Thank them. Keep it kind, even when it is the third message.
  • Keep a note of what you sent and when.

Make the next invoice easier to collect.

Put your payment terms on every quote, so nobody is surprised. Send the invoice as soon as the job is done, while it is fresh. Show the due date clearly, and make paying a one-step job.

If an invoice stays unpaid after your final message, speak to your accountant or a legal adviser about the options open to you.

Quick answers

Quick answers.

When should I send the first reminder?

The day after the due date is fine. Keep it light and assume it slipped through.

Should I phone or write?

Write first so there is a record. If it goes unanswered, phone. A short call often clears things up.
