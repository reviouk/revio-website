The problem

You did not start a business to spend Sunday night on admin.

Owners of small UK businesses lose around eight hours a week to admin. Most of it is the same five jobs: answering the phone while on another job, replying to enquiries, chasing invoices, writing quotes and keeping the diary straight. None of it is the work you are paid for, and all of it piles up the moment you are busy.

Half of small firms have tried AI. Most stopped at a chat window, because the hard part is not the typing. The hard part is connecting an assistant to your phone, your inbox, your diary, your invoicing and your customer list, and teaching it how you work. That is the job we do. You get Flo, already holding the keys.

What Flo does

Five jobs Flo takes off you in the first month.

Answers the phone

When you can't pick up, Flo does. Flo takes the details, answers the usual questions and books the job in your diary. You get a summary by text.

Clears the inbox

Sorts what matters from what does not, drafts replies in your voice for you to approve, and chases the people who have gone quiet.

Chases the money

Watches your invoices and sends polite reminders at 7, 14 and 21 days, in your tone, until they are paid. You see every one before it goes.

Keeps the diary

Books, moves and confirms appointments, writes up meeting notes and sends the follow-up you would have forgotten.

Drafts the quote

Turns a phone call or an email into a quote in your format in minutes, priced from your own list, ready for you to check and send.

Remembers every customer

Who they are, what they asked for, what you quoted and when you last spoke. One place, on your phone, so nothing goes cold.

Straight answers

Questions owners ask before they book.

Is Flo a real person?

No. Flo is the name we give the assistant so you and your customers have something to call it. If a caller asks, Flo says it is an automated assistant and offers to take a message or book a slot. Anything it is unsure about goes to you.

Is this just a chatbot?

No. A chatbot waits for you to type. Flo is connected to your phone, inbox, diary, invoicing and customer list, knows your prices and your tone, and does the jobs on a schedule whether or not you are at a screen. The connecting and the teaching are what we do for the setup fee.

Will Flo send things without me seeing them?

Not until you say so. For the first fortnight every call summary, draft reply, quote and reminder goes past you. After that you choose which jobs Flo may do alone, for example invoice reminders, and which still need your approval, for example quotes. Payments, contracts and anything legal always need you.

What does the monthly plan cover?

Flo's running costs, monitoring so we know when something breaks before you do, fixes, and one change a month as your business shifts. Solo starts at From £125, Team at From £245 and Business at From £595, all excluding VAT. Cancel any time. You keep the accounts and the data.

Do I need to change my software?

Almost never. We connect to the phone, email, calendar, invoicing and customer tools you already use. If you have no customer list at all, we set up a simple one as part of the package. We name no software on these pages because the point is that we fit around yours.

Where does my data go?

Flo reads your email, calls and invoices, so this matters. Every account is in your name, data stays in your tools, and the AI services we use process it under business terms, not consumer ones. You get a plain-English one-page note on what it can see and where it goes, and you can disconnect anything at any time.

How quickly is it live?

Solo in about a week, Team in two to three weeks, Business in three to four. The first fortnight after that is supervised, so expect the full benefit about a month after you sign.

What if I am bigger than 40 staff?

Then the right first step is the AI Opportunity Audit at From £1,500: two weeks to find where AI pays across several departments, with governance and reporting built in. These packages are deliberately for smaller businesses.

Could I set this up myself?

If you are comfortable with software and have a spare weekend, yes, and the result will be a useful chat tool. What you will probably not do is wire it into your phone line, your invoicing and your diary, write the rules for what it may do alone, and keep it running when something changes. That is what you are paying for.
