Where this fits

The paperwork moves as much as the freight.

A haulage firm, courier or freight forwarder runs on documents as much as on trucks: a quote request that arrives as a scruffy email, a proof of delivery photographed on a driver's phone, a customer asking where their load is, and a stack of compliance paperwork that has to be right every time. None of that requires a person to decide anything clever, it requires someone to read, extract and reply fast, which is exactly what AI does well. Quoting from emails, POD and document extraction, driver and customer comms, and compliance paperwork are the four jobs we build first in this sector.

What we do not automate is the judgement calls: which carrier to use on a tight lane, whether to accept a load at a marginal rate, how to handle a damaged consignment. Those stay with your ops team. The AI's job is to get the paperwork off their desk fast enough that they have time to make those calls properly, rather than to make the calls for them.

Where AI pays

Six places transport operators see it earn its keep.

Not a platform. Each of these is a defined, working thing on your own systems.

Quoting from emails

A rate request lands as a free-text email, and a first-pass quote comes back in minutes, built from your rate card and past jobs, ready for a human to check before it is sent.

POD and document extraction

Proof of delivery, CMR notes and delivery photos read automatically and keyed into the job record, so nobody re-types a signature or a delivery time from a phone photo.

Driver communications

Drivers get their next job, a route change or a delay notice by message rather than a phone call mid-shift, and can reply with a status update the system reads and logs.

Customer status updates

A customer asking "where is my load" gets an answer from the live tracking and job data, not a call transferred round the office while someone finds the file.

Load planning support

A first-pass plan for which loads go together, drafted from capacity, postcode and time-window data, checked by the planner rather than built from a blank sheet.

Compliance paperwork

Driver hours records, vehicle checks and licence renewals drafted and flagged before they lapse, so compliance is chased automatically instead of discovered late.

Straight answers

Questions transport operators ask before they start.

Will this replace our TMS?

No. It sits alongside the system you already run, reading and writing into it. We do not propose ripping out a working TMS to install AI; the automation connects to it.

Can AI actually read a scanned POD or a driver photo?

Yes, document extraction from photographs, scans and PDFs is one of the most reliable uses of AI available today, and it is usually the fastest-paying automation we build in this sector.

What happens if the AI misreads a quote or a document?

Every draft is checked by a person before it goes to a customer or into a compliance record during the supervised launch period, and flagged exceptions route to a human by default even after that.

Do drivers need to learn a new app?

Usually not. Driver comms are built on the messaging channel they already use, WhatsApp or SMS most often, rather than a new piece of software to install.

How does this handle multiple depots or a mixed fleet?

The audit maps this specifically. Multi-site operations usually mean one automation built once and rolled out, with local rate cards or rules configured per depot.

What about driver hours and licence compliance?

The system drafts and flags, it does not decide compliance on your behalf. A named person still signs off every record; the automation just stops anything falling through unnoticed.

How fast could a first automation be live?

A first build, usually quoting or POD extraction, is typically live within six weeks of the audit finishing, running unsupervised within nine.
