What to post on social media when you run a small business.

A simple weekly pattern, so Monday morning does not start with a blank box.

More from the blog

You know your trade. Knowing what to say about it in a post is a different skill. A simple pattern helps. Decide once what each post of the week is for, and most of the question answers itself.

This pattern suits a sole trader or a small team. It works on Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn or your Google Business Profile.

A simple week of three posts.

Start with three posts a week. That is easy to keep up, and keeping up matters more than posting a lot.

Post one: show the work. Share a photo of a job, a finished piece or something you are making. Add a sentence or two on what it was and who it was for, with their permission.

Post two: be useful. Answer a question customers ask you all the time. A short tip, a simple checklist, or what to do before you call. Once you know the question, the post nearly writes itself.

Post three: invite people in. Say what you do, where you work and how to get in touch. Mention when you have space, or when the diary is filling up.

An example, so you can see it.

Say you run a dog grooming business. This is only an example.

Show the work: "Before and after for a very muddy spaniel. We both had a good morning."
Be useful: "How often should you brush a long coat? Here is what we tell our regulars."
Invite: "A few appointments free next week. Message us to book."

Keep it in your own voice.

Write the way you would talk to a customer on the phone. Short sentences. One idea in each post. Real photos from your own work look better than stock pictures, and they are easier to take than you think.

Only say what is true. Do not promise an offer you cannot honour, and do not quote a price unless you are sure of it. If you mention a customer, ask first.

Make it easy on yourself.

Take photos as you work, even if you never use them. Keep a note on your phone of the questions customers ask. Those notes become your "be useful" posts.

Sit down once a week and write or choose the week's posts in one go. Pick the network where your customers already are. One network you keep up beats four you neglect. If you use two, the same post often works on both with a small change.

When you are short of ideas.

Some weeks nothing happens that seems worth posting. That is fine. Look back at your recent jobs for something to show. Think of the last three questions a customer asked. Mention the season, such as what to do before winter or ahead of a bank holiday. Say thank you to a supplier, or to the people who send you work. These are all honest posts, and none needs much time.

Check before you post.

  • Is the photo clear, and do you have permission to show anyone or anything in it?
  • Are the prices, dates and opening times right?
  • Is your phone number or link on the post, or easy to find on the page?
  • Does it sound like you?
Quick answers

Quick answers.

How often should I post?

Pick a number you can keep up. Three a week is a good place to start. You can add more later, or drop to two in a busy month.

Do I need a photo every time?

Not always, but a real photo from your own work feels personal. Even a quick phone photo will do.
