The size of business we mean

Big enough to feel the pain, too small for a big consultancy's minimum.

Between roughly £1m and £25m turnover, a business is usually past the point where the founder can personally answer every enquiry and check every invoice, and well short of having a data team or a Head of AI to work it out. That gap is where most of the wasted AI spend happens: a chatbot bought because a competitor has one, a tool trialled by one enthusiastic manager and abandoned when they leave, a subscription nobody remembers approving.

The businesses that get value from AI at this size are the ones that treat it like any other investment: find the process that actually costs money or time today, fix that one thing properly, prove it pays, then move to the next. Everything else on this page is that method, applied.

What actually pays at this size

Four places the money usually is.

Enquiry handling

Website, DM and phone enquiries answered, qualified and booked without a person copying details between five systems. Usually the single biggest win.

Quoting

Quotes and proposals drafted from your own past work in minutes rather than hours, checked by a human before they go out.

Admin

Invoices, applications and forms read and keyed into the systems that need them, instead of a person retyping the same fields all day.

Reporting

A weekly number a director can actually read, produced automatically instead of assembled by hand from four spreadsheets on a Friday afternoon.

What to ignore

What usually is not worth the money at this size.

Worth doing first

  • Enquiry handling and quoting, where the volume is highest
  • CRM hygiene, so the rest of the system has something to work from
  • One agent or automation, proven, before a second is started

Usually not yet

  • A general-purpose chatbot with no defined job to do
  • A platform build before a single process has proven the case
  • Replacing the CRM instead of using the one you already have

Fit

Who this is for, and who it is not.

A good fit

  • UK business turning over roughly £1m to £25m
  • A CRM already in place, however untidy
  • A specific process that is visibly slow, expensive or error-prone
  • A director able to spend an hour reviewing progress

Not this, yet

  • You are pre-revenue or under about £1m turnover, where a smaller off-the-shelf tool is usually the better first step

Yes, though the first job is usually getting one in place properly rather than building AI on top of spreadsheets. The audit will say so if that is the real first step.

Is £1,500 really the whole audit cost?

Yes, fixed, no day rate, and it comes off the first build if you go ahead within the audit's recommendation window.

What if the audit finds nothing worth doing?

That happens occasionally, and we say so. You have then spent £1,500 finding out AI is not your next priority, which is a useful answer in itself.

How fast will we see something working?

Two weeks for the audit, then typically two to six weeks for the first build to ship, depending on its size.

Do we need technical people on our side?

No. Most SME clients have no technical team at all. We work with whoever runs the process day to day and build for them.

Will you tell us if AI is the wrong answer?

Yes. A lower-cost piece of off-the-shelf software or simply fixing a broken process is sometimes the real fix, and we say so rather than building something to justify the fee.
